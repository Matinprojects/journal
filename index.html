Where Your Journal Starts

A broker statement holds the whole life of the account. If you're starting a fresh campaign today, you don't want years of old trades landing in it. Set a start date and everything before it is read, counted, and left out — every time, automatically, no matter how often you drop the same file in.

Import TradesDuplicates are skipped automatically

Two ways in — whichever suits you. Trades are matched on position ticket, so you can drop the whole history file every single day without creating duplicates.

Option 1 · A file from your platform

Works with the JournalExport script output, this app's own CSV export, a broker CSV, or the HTML report MetaTrader saves from History → Report. Reports containing several tables let you choose which one to read.

Option 2 · Paste from your history table

In MetaTrader: History tab → right-click → Positions → click inside the table → CtrlA then CtrlC → paste below. Works with any platform that lets you copy a table.

Option 3 · Screenshot

Take a screenshot of your history table and drop it here — or just press CtrlV anywhere on this page after copying it. The text is read out of the image and lands in the box above, where you can correct anything before importing.

◔
Reading text from an image is never perfect. Small figures on a dark platform theme are the hardest case. Always look over the preview before importing — and if a screenshot fights you, copying the table as text instead is both faster and exact.

A box measures a batch of trades. A month measures your life. Both matter, and they rarely line up — which is why they are tracked separately.

Start a New Box

A box is a fixed number of trades you commit to before judging anything. Decide the rules once, then spend the box obeying them — that is what makes the statistics at the end mean something. Trades are attached to the open box automatically, in date order, until it fills.

Monthly Performance

Session Windows

Your own map of the day, with your real results laid over it. An indicator can tell you which windows the market tends to move in; only your journal can tell you which of them actually pay you. Where those two disagree is the most useful thing on this page.

Cross Analysisany two dimensions

One dimension rarely tells the truth on its own. A strategy is not simply good or bad — it is good somewhere. Put strategy against your time blocks and the answer usually appears in a single glance.

What If

Switch anything off and watch the curve redraw. This is the cheapest experiment in trading: it costs nothing to find out that a whole category of trades has been quietly paying for itself with your other trades' profits.

Discipline Scorenothing to do with profit

Profit is partly the market's decision. This is entirely yours — it measures only whether you did what you said you would. A trader with a rising discipline score and a flat equity curve is in a far better position than the reverse.

Session Windows

Your time map, marked against your own results. Each window carries the weight you gave it — the question this answers is whether the market agrees. A window can only be judged once it has enough trades, so anything still thin is shown as such rather than guessed at.

Monte Carlo

Your edge is a coin with a bias, and a run of trades is a sequence of flips. This shuffles your own results thousands of times to show the range of futures that same edge can produce — not the one you hope for.

Money

Numbers & Thresholds

How much data the analysis demands before it will draw a conclusion, and the risk limits printed into your draft plan.

Trade Gate Rules

The gate reads these before every trade. Set a limit to zero to switch it off. The lockout is the only part of this app that stops you doing something — it exists because the moment you most want to ignore your rules is the moment they're worth the most.

Payout Guards

The share you withdraw is set per box. These are the rules that sit on top of it and decide whether taking it is wise this particular week. Set any to zero to switch it off.

That last one is how funds pay themselves, and it is the rule most private traders are missing. Without it you withdraw on the bounce after a losing run, and the account never gets back to where it was.

Session Map — Share and Reuse

Your time windows travel as a small open file. Send it to another trader, load it on a second machine, or keep versions of a map you're testing. Anyone can read the format — that's the point.
